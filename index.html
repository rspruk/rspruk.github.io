The method

History, re-run without the shock

A synthetic control builds a weighted composite of comparable countries that tracks the treated country before an event. After the event, the gap between the two lines is the estimated cost or dividend of that event.

The same logic runs through papers on the 1979 Iranian Revolution, Syria's civil war, Danish trans-fat rules and Spain's devolution.

    Featured software

    Exposure-augmented synthetic control · Stata

    Synthetic Control under Out-of-Span Common Shocks: Diagnosis, Exposure Balance and Correction

    Standard synthetic control assumes the treated unit's untreated path can be rebuilt from its donor pool. When a common shock hits the treated unit with an exposure no donor combination can reproduce, that assumption fails. This paper shows how to diagnose the problem, balance donors on exposure, and correct the estimate. The application is Iran's confrontation with the West.

    Rok Spruk · working paper, 2026 · application in arXiv 2602.03231

          About
          
        
          An economist of institutions and their breakdowns
          
        
          
          
          Rok Spruk is Associate Professor of Economics at the School of Economics and Business, University of Ljubljana, and Adjunct Research Fellow at the University of Western Australia. He earned his PhD in quantitative economic history at Utrecht University in 2016 under Jan Luiten van Zanden, with a thesis on institutions, growth and long-run development paths.
          
          
          His work estimates the long-run economic consequences of revolutions, wars, constitutional change, judicial reform and regulation, mostly with synthetic control and other counterfactual methods. He has held visiting positions at Bucerius Law School in Hamburg and at EconomiX, CNRS – Université Paris Nanterre, and advises the European Commission's DG Regional and Urban Policy on regional policy evaluation; he wrote the synthetic control study for the Commission's ex post evaluation of the 2014–2020 Cohesion Policy.
          
          
          He serves as Associate Editor of the International Review of Law and Economics and Acta Oeconomica.
